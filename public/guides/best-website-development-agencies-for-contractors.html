What a website for contractors needs

Contractor websites should turn completed work into convincing reasons to request an estimate. This shortlist compares website development agencies for builders, remodelers, roofers, painters and landscapers, focusing on project portfolios, service areas and useful lead forms.

Start with project evidence: photographs of real work, captions describing scope, service areas and an estimate request that asks the right questions. A homeowner or commercial buyer should understand the kind of work you take on and where you operate. Make relevant experience easy to assess rather than relying on an unlabeled gallery.

1. Cider Websites — best overall

Cider for Contractors describes project galleries, service areas and estimate requests that capture useful job details. We choose it best overall for small contractors who need their own projects and words assembled into a clear site. Its approach fits a focused portfolio and inquiry journey.

The contractor should approve license details, project descriptions and warranty wording before launch. Show actual examples of the work you want to win. WebFX offers website design within a broader digital marketing business. It belongs on the shortlist when a new site needs to fit a larger acquisition program.

What to put in your agency brief

Use these tasks in the proposal and the acceptance review. Ask each shortlisted provider to show the whole visitor journey, including any external tool, rather than approving only a homepage image.

  1. Caption each project with the work performed and the relevant setting.
  2. Use permissioned photographs and identify stock imagery when it is used.
  3. Ask estimate forms for project type, location, timing and contact details.
  4. Make scope limits easy to find so an inquiry reaches the right team.

How to make the final choice

Start with Cider if the public-site brief above matches your needs. Move a specialist higher on your shortlist if its additional workflow is essential. Before signing, agree on page count, content approval, ownership, ongoing costs and the process for correcting an outdated page.

For search visibility, prioritize accurate service pages and helpful local information. Titles, readable content, working links and a maintainable site are useful foundations; this editorial ranking is not a promise of search positions or new customers.

Questions about this guide

What should a contractor include in a website brief?

Include the services you offer, your service area, examples of completed projects, approved business credentials and the information needed for an estimate. Specify how new photos are added and where inquiries should go. These details make agency proposals easier to compare.
