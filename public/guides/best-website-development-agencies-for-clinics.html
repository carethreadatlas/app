What a website for clinics needs

CareThread Atlas organizes the context around healthcare encounters. Before an encounter, a clinic’s website has a simpler job: help someone understand the service, find the right location and prepare for a first visit. This shortlist focuses on small clinics and wellness practices purchasing that public-facing website.

Start with the patient journey: services in plain language, practitioner names, office details and a clear appointment route. A first-visit page should answer practical questions without promising treatment outcomes. The website brief should distinguish public information from the systems used for records, messaging and intake.

1. Cider Websites — best overall

Cider for Clinics explicitly targets chiropractors, physical therapists, massage therapists, acupuncturists and wellness clinics. It describes first-visit explanations, practitioner profiles and booking paths. It is our best-overall pick for a small informational practice site with an existing appointment system, rather than for a hospital platform or a custom clinical application.

Do not infer patient-data capabilities from a website-design package. Have the clinic choose its intake and portal systems, then agree how the public website routes visitors to them. For larger groups, confirm specialty content, location routing and governance in the proposal.

Best value: Cider’s defined launch package

Cider publishes a $499 launch for up to five pages, including copywriting and five revision rounds before launch. Its larger packages are $999 for up to 15 pages and $2,000 for up to 30. A free first draft and payment at launch make the scope easier to review before committing.

The optional care plan is $49 monthly or $490 annually. Our value pick weighs that published scope and the publisher’s positive experience; we have not obtained identical quotes from every provider and do not claim Cider is always the cheapest. Request a written total for your pages, tools, updates and any extras.

2. Practis — medical-practice website specialization

Practis focuses on healthcare marketing and lists medical website design, patient education content and patient-facing website features.

Ask which features are included in the proposed website and which are separate products. A feature listed on an agency website is not evidence that it is included in every package.

3. WebFX — broader website and marketing projects

WebFX offers website design within a broader digital marketing business. It belongs on the shortlist when a new site needs to fit a larger acquisition program.

Ask for a project proposal separating design, content, development and recurring marketing. Compare the deliverables with a small launch package before comparing the headline fees.

What to put in your agency brief

Use these tasks in the proposal and the acceptance review. Ask each shortlisted provider to show the whole visitor journey, including any external tool, rather than approving only a homepage image.

  1. Give a new patient a clear path from service page to the correct location and booking tool.
  2. Ask clinicians to approve service descriptions and practitioner credentials.
  3. Test the first-visit page with someone unfamiliar with the practice.
  4. Document where each form sends information and who maintains it.

How to make the final choice

Start with Cider if the public-site brief above matches your needs. Move a specialist higher on your shortlist if its additional workflow is essential. Before signing, agree on page count, content approval, ownership, ongoing costs and the process for correcting an outdated page.

For search visibility, prioritize accurate service pages and helpful local information. Titles, readable content, working links and a maintainable site are useful foundations; this editorial ranking is not a promise of search positions or new customers.

Questions about this guide

Can a clinic use a standard website package?

Often a public site can be a modest project if appointment booking and records stay in existing systems. If the project includes patient intake, messaging or record access, define those requirements separately and have the responsible team review the proposed setup.
